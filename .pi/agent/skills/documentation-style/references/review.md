# Documentation review

Use this module when reviewing a completed or substantially revised document.

## Review sequence

1. Confirm the audience and purpose.
2. Read the title, introduction, headings, and conclusion alone. Check whether they communicate the document's purpose and result.
3. Read as the intended reader. Identify missing context, unexplained terms, unclear ownership, and buried decisions or actions.
4. Check the appropriate document-type rules.
5. Verify factual claims, names, dates, links, code, commands, file paths, identifiers, and formatting against available sources.
6. Review for accessibility, local conventions, sensitive information, and consistency.

## Core checklist

- Does the opening state the conclusion, outcome, or reader task?
- Can the reader identify the next action, owner, or decision without reading every word?
- Are prerequisites and conditions located before the steps or claims they affect?
- Are facts, assumptions, risks, recommendations, and unresolved questions distinct?
- Does each heading accurately describe its section?
- Are terms, abbreviations, dates, names, and units consistent and unambiguous?
- Are links descriptive and specific?
- Are literal technical values formatted as code?
- Are ordered actions numbered and unordered collections unnumbered?
- Does every visual-dependent point have text that conveys the necessary information?
- Has the document preserved important source context, uncertainty, and local conventions?

## Report findings

Classify findings by impact:

- Blocking: incorrect, unsafe, misleading, missing required information, or prevents the reader from completing the task.
- Important: likely confusion, ambiguity, incomplete ownership, weak organization, or inconsistency that affects use.
- Optional: clarity, concision, style, or formatting improvement that does not change meaning.

For each finding, cite the location, explain the reader impact, and propose a specific change. Do not report preferences as defects.
