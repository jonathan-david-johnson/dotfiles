# Tone and language

Use this module for rewrites, reader-facing documents, terminology choices, jargon, ambiguity, and tone review.

## Tone

- Be professional, direct, respectful, and natural.
- Prefer useful information over performance, humor, marketing language, or excessive formality.
- Avoid slang, cultural references, cliches, filler phrases, and unnecessary intensifiers.
- Avoid placeholder phrases such as "please note," "at this time," and "it is important to note that." State the relevant fact instead.
- Do not describe a procedure as easy, simple, quick, or obvious. Explain the work and its conditions.
- Do not use "we" when it obscures who is responsible. Name the team, system, role, or owner if that distinction matters.
- Use "you" in instructions when the reader is the actor. Do not force second person in reports, status updates, decision records, or meeting notes.

## Clarity

- Prefer specific nouns and verbs over abstract wording.
- Prefer "The deployment failed because the signing certificate expired" to "There was an issue with deployment."
- Prefer "Maria approves the release" to "The release is approved," unless the approver is irrelevant.
- Replace an ambiguous time reference, such as "recently" or "soon," with a date, window, or explicit uncertainty where possible.
- Introduce abbreviations only when they recur or the audience knows them. Define them on first use when needed.
- Use one spelling and name for a concept throughout the document.

## Evidence and uncertainty

- Label observed facts as facts.
- Label interpretations and recommendations as such.
- State the evidence and limitation when confidence is material.
- Preserve open questions. Do not fill gaps with plausible but unverified details.

## Global audience and accessibility

- Use plain language that is likely to translate well.
- Avoid idioms, metaphors, sarcasm, and culture-dependent references when clarity matters.
- Do not use color alone to convey meaning.
- Use headings that describe the section's content.
